## Can I Bring a Frame I Bought Online to a Physical Store for Lens Fitting? Yes.

Short answer first: **yes.** A new frame bought online can be brought to an optical shop for lens fitting — it's something we do regularly.

This question usually comes up because a frame and its lenses are actually two separate things. What you get from an online order is mostly an "empty frame" — you paid for the look, the material, the brand, but the two clear pieces inside are often display lenses with no prescription, sometimes even plastic placeholders. Turning that into a pair of glasses you can actually wear clearly and comfortably takes one more step: **measurement, and the craft of precisely fitting a lens into that specific frame.**

That's exactly the step a physical store fills in.

| | What an online frame gives you | What in-store lens fitting adds |
|---|---|---|
| Shape and material | ✅ The frame you picked | — |
| Prescription lens | ❌ Usually a display lens with no prescription | ✅ Selected and fitted to your prescription and needs |
| Pupillary distance / fitting height measurement | ❌ Not included | ✅ Full measurement |
| Vertex distance / frame fit | ❌ Not included | ✅ Adjusted on-site to fit |
| Lens fitting craftsmanship | ❌ Not included | ✅ Professional fitting |

To be clear upfront: **we're not saying buying online is a bad idea.** You already found a frame you like, and the money you saved on the frame is real savings. What we do is help turn that frame into a pair of glasses you can actually wear — connecting the piece that needs professional expertise the most.

## Why Does "Just Getting Lenses Fitted" Actually Need More Measurement, Not Less?

A lot of people assume that once the frame's picked out, fitting the lens is just a small matter of dropping glass into the frame. It's actually the opposite.

Whether a lens performs correctly is only half about the prescription — **the other half is the measurement.** For the same prescription, whether the lens's optical center aligns with your pupil, how far the lens sits from your eye (vertex distance), the tilt angle of the frame on your face, and where the fitting height lands — if any of these are off even slightly, the glasses can feel "off," a little dizzy, or leave your eyes strained after extended wear. Even with the correct prescription, a misaligned optical center still won't feel right.

That's why at Beyond Visual Optometry, **bring-your-own-frame and in-store-selected frames go through the exact same standard process**: a full eye exam, plus complete measurement of pupillary distance, fitting height, vertex distance, and frame fit — nothing skipped. Measurements don't get shortened just because you brought your own frame. For anyone who spends long hours on screens, how thorough this measurement step is often makes the difference between "comfortable all day" and "wanting to take them off within the hour."

Assembly is also a craft in itself. Rimless frames, semi-rimless frames, or anything with more demanding construction require careful decisions about how the lens gets drilled, seated, and secured — all of which affect how stable the finished product is. This is detailed work that combines machine precision with hand skill, and it's exactly where "dedicated lens fitting" earns its value.

## Can Any Frame Be Fitted? The Optometrist Checks the Frame's Condition First

New frames, designer frames, worn old frames, secondhand or vintage frames — all welcome. But before we start, **the optometrist will check the frame's condition and material** — this step protects your frame; it's not a hurdle we're putting up.

Here's the practical reason: disassembling, drilling, and fitting a frame inherently puts some stress on it. New frames are usually fine, but frames that are aged, well-worn, or naturally more brittle carry a real risk of damage during the process. Our approach is:

1. The optometrist evaluates the frame's condition first and determines whether it's suitable for fitting.
2. If the risk is higher, **we'll tell you honestly** upfront, and we proceed only once you understand and agree.
3. If, after evaluation, it's genuinely not suitable, we'll recommend against it directly, so we don't risk damaging a frame you care about.

Bottom line: **we proceed only after evaluation and your agreement.** Saying this upfront is how we take responsibility for your frame — and for you.
