## Can I Bring My Own Frame and Just Get Lenses Fitted?

**Yes — and this is exactly what we do every day.**

A lot of people hesitate right at the door: "I didn't buy this frame here, I just want lenses put in it — is that awkward to ask?" We understand that hesitation completely. Let's say it upfront: at Beyond Visual Optometry, a bring-your-own-frame lens fitting isn't some kind of favor we're doing you — it's a service we perform every day. A new frame you ordered online, a designer frame you've held onto for years, a familiar old frame, even a vintage frame — all welcome.

That "awkward" feeling usually comes from one misunderstanding: assuming optical shops make their money selling frames, so skipping the frame purchase means wasting our time. But how comfortable and clear a pair of glasses feels comes down mostly to **the lens itself and the fitting measurements** — the frame is really just the structure that holds the lens steadily in front of your eyes. Getting the lens right is the core of what a fitting is.

## Clearing Up the Common Misconceptions About "Just Getting Lenses Fitted"

| What you might assume | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| You'll get charged extra because the frame's from elsewhere | Cost = lens price + a fixed fitting fee — no surcharge for outside frames |
| The eye exam will be rushed since you're only getting lenses | The exam and measurements follow the exact same standard as a full pair — nothing skipped |
| Bringing your own frame is a "small job" shops don't want | This is a dedicated service of ours — online orders, old frames, secondhand frames, all accepted |
| Any frame can just have lenses dropped in | The optometrist evaluates the frame's condition first to confirm it can be safely fitted |

One more note on that last row: evaluating the frame isn't there to make things difficult — it's there to protect your frame. Disassembling and fitting a frame always carries some risk of damage, so checking the material and structure first, and confirming it can hold up, is how we avoid damaging a frame you brought in — especially one with real value or sentimental history.

## Before You Come In, You Just Need Two Things

Bringing your own frame for a lens fitting is simple. Just bring:

- **The frame you want fitted** — a new online order, a designer frame, an old frame, a secondhand or vintage frame — all fine. If the frame has visible looseness, cracks, or warping, just let us know when you bring it in.
- **A "prescription reference"** — the easiest option is bringing your current glasses so we can measure the prescription directly as a starting point. A recent prescription record also works well. If you have neither, that's fine too — we'll do a full exam from scratch.

No old glasses, no prescription record? No problem at all. A full eye exam always reconfirms your current prescription — this step doesn't get skipped just because you're only getting lenses fitted.

## The In-Store Process for Bring-Your-Own-Frame Lens Fitting

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Frame evaluation | The optometrist checks the frame's material, structure, and wear before confirming it's suitable for fitting |
| 2. Risk disclosure | If there's any risk of damage during fitting, we'll say so upfront; we proceed only with your understanding and agreement, and we'll recommend against fitting a frame that's not up to it |
| 3. Full eye exam | A complete exam based on your visual needs, confirming your current prescription |
| 4. Fitting measurements | Pupillary distance, fitting height, vertex distance, and frame fit are each measured individually — aligning the prescription to your eyes, not just fitting the lens into the frame |
| 5. Lens selection and quote | Lens options explained based on your needs and budget (we're an authorized ZEISS and HOYA dealer); cost = lens price + fixed fitting fee, quoted clearly |
| 6. Production and follow-up adjustment | Try the finished glasses on in store to confirm fit; if the frame shifts afterward, come back anytime for an adjustment |

See steps 3 and 4? **That's where the real work of a lens fitting happens.** For the same prescription, whether the pupillary distance is correct, whether the lens's optical center lands on your line of sight, and what the vertex distance is between the frame and your eyes — these make a real difference in clarity and comfort. Where you bought the frame has nothing to do with whether these measurements get done — none of them get skipped.

## FAQ

**Q1: Will it really not cost more if I only get lenses fitted?**

No surcharge for bringing your own frame. The cost is "lens price + a fixed fitting fee" — the lens is priced by the brand and tier you choose, and the fitting fee is a flat amount. You can get a quote by visiting in person or messaging us on LINE before deciding.

**Q2: I don't have old glasses and don't know my prescription — can I still just get lenses fitted?**

Yes. A full eye exam will reconfirm your current prescription — you don't need to know it beforehand. Just bring the frame you want fitted; if you happen to have your current glasses on hand, bringing those along helps as a reference, but it's not required.

**Q3: Is the exam less thorough if I bring my own frame?**

No. The exam and measurement standards are exactly the same whether you bring your own frame or choose one in store — a full eye exam plus pupillary distance, fitting height, vertex distance, and frame fit. Measurement accuracy is most of what makes a lens fit right, and that's exactly what a dedicated lens fitting focuses on.

**Q4: Can any frame just get lenses fitted directly?**

It needs an evaluation first. Online orders, designer frames, old frames, secondhand and vintage frames are all welcome, but the optometrist checks the frame's condition and material first to confirm it can be safely fitted. If there's risk, we'll disclose it and proceed only with your agreement; if a frame really isn't suitable, we'll say so honestly rather than push ahead.
