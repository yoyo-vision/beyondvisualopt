## Eye Strain and Fatigue: Triage First — See an Ophthalmologist if You Have These Warning Signs, Otherwise Let Us Help Find the Cause

Let's say this upfront. If your eyes feel sore, tight, or tired along with redness, pain, a sudden drop in vision, or shadows or flashes in your vision, your body is telling you "it's time to see a physician" — please see an ophthalmologist as soon as possible, don't wait it out. But if it's more like "I've been staring at a screen all day, and by evening my eyes are sore and dry and I just want to close them," that's usually not directly related to eye health — it's your visual system running "overtime." In that case, coming in for a full visual assessment at an optometry practice can usually identify the cause.

Let's be clear about who we are: Beyond Visual Optometry is a licensed optometry practice (Beyond Visual Optometry / Biyang Optometry), not an ophthalmology clinic, we don't have a physician on site, and we don't diagnose or treat eye disease. What we can do is take "why do my eyes get tired every time I use them" and break it down from a visual perspective.

### 30-Second Triage: Where Should Your Situation Go?

| Your Situation | Recommendation |
|---------|------|
| Red, swollen, painful eyes, discharge, light sensitivity | See an ophthalmologist as soon as possible |
| Sudden drop in vision, one eye unclear, part of your visual field missing | See an ophthalmologist as soon as possible |
| A sudden increase in floaters, along with flashes | See an ophthalmologist as soon as possible |
| Sore, tight, dry eyes after long use, just wanting to close your eyes | Come in for a full visual assessment at an optometry practice |
| Switching between distance and near feels harder, especially tired by evening | Come in for a full visual assessment at an optometry practice |
| New glasses actually make you more tired or dizzy | Come in and let us check your prescription and fitting parameters |

Eyes get sore from long use, and a lot of people assume "resting will fix it" — but if it happens every single day and rest doesn't help, there are often a few visual causes behind it: your prescription doesn't match your current visual demands (your distance prescription is spot-on, but you spend all day looking near), your two eyes are working hard to stay coordinated (a small issue in how they focus and converge together), or presbyopia is quietly setting in and near work is getting harder. None of these are diseases — they're visual conditions, and that's exactly what an optometrist is equipped to handle.

To be straightforward: we don't "treat fatigue," and there's no pair of glasses that "eliminates" fatigue. What we do is assess and find the cause — going through your visual habits, prescription, and binocular coordination to see if there's anything adjustable on the visual side. If everything measures normal and the fatigue has another cause, or if we find during the process that your vision is abnormal and can't be adequately corrected, we'll refer you to an ophthalmologist under Taiwan's Optometrists Act rather than pushing ahead with glasses. Telling you "this needs to be seen by a physician" is part of doing this professionally.

## FAQ

**Q1. Does eye soreness always mean I should see an ophthalmologist first?**

It depends on whether there are warning signs. If the soreness comes with redness, swelling, noticeable pain, discharge, light sensitivity, or a sudden drop in vision, please go straight to an ophthalmologist — these fall under eye disease and need a physician's diagnosis. If it's just soreness and fatigue after long use with none of the above, you can come to an optometry practice first for a full visual assessment, starting with your prescription and binocular coordination. If you're really not sure, describe your situation to us over LINE first and we'll tell you honestly where to go.

**Q2. What can an optometry practice actually do for my eye fatigue?**

We don't provide treatment, and we won't claim that any pair of glasses eliminates fatigue. What an optometrist does is assess and find the cause: measuring your prescription, reviewing your visual habits, and checking binocular coordination to see if there's anything adjustable on the visual side. Sometimes the prescription needs updating, sometimes your working distance doesn't match your lens design. Once the fitting parameters are dialed in correctly, using your eyes naturally feels less strained — but that's matching your visual conditions correctly, not treatment.

**Q3. I stare at a screen a lot — is eye soreness normal? Do I need to do anything about it?**

Some soreness after short-term use that eases with rest is common. But if it's sore every single day and rest doesn't really help, or it's affecting your work, it's worth getting a visual assessment — especially for jobs involving long hours of near work, where prescription and binocular coordination details are easy to overlook. An assessment doesn't necessarily mean you need new glasses — we might just point out an adjustment to your visual habits and working distance.

**Q4. If the exam shows it's not a prescription issue, what happens next?**

If the measurement comes back normal and the fatigue isn't coming from the visual side, or if your vision is abnormal and can't be adequately corrected with lenses, we'll recommend a referral to an ophthalmologist under Taiwan's Optometrists Act for further evaluation. An optometry practice's responsibility isn't just fitting you with glasses — it also includes honestly pointing you toward the right door when you need to see a physician.
