## A Long List of Names, Sorted Into Four Groups by Use

"Balansis, MyStyle, Lifestyle — which one is which?" (In Taiwan you'll also see them under Chinese trade names such as 金鑽, 銀鑽 and 博銳視.) Lay HOYA's product names out side by side and most people get stuck right there the first time. The names come from marketing; the way the range is organized is actually simple. Sort the series by use first — single vision, progressive, indoor and reading, driving — and within each group they step up by design tier.

HOYA started out in Japan in 1941. Its original business was optical glass; eyeglass lenses came later. ## HOYA Single-Vision Lenses: Nulux and Hilux

A single-vision lens has one power across the whole lens, and most everyday glasses for myopia, hyperopia and astigmatism are this type. HOYA grades its single-vision lenses by surface design:

### Double-Sided Aspheric and Personalized: Nulux V+, EP

According to the manufacturer's published information, lenses in this tier have curvature designed into both the front and back surfaces, with the surface curve changing gradually from the center to the edge. They come in refractive indices of 1.60, 1.67 and 1.74. For how spherical, aspheric and double-sided aspheric designs differ, see the table in [What Is an "Aspheric" Lens?](/en/blog/aspheric-vs-spherical-lens/)

### Aspheric and Spherical: Nulux Classic, Hilux

Nulux Classic is an aspheric design and Hilux is a spherical design, with refractive indices from 1.50 to 1.74. If your prescription isn't strong and your frame isn't large, this tier is usually where you start looking.

## HOYA Progressive Lenses: From Standard Designs to Personalized

In a progressive lens the power changes gradually from top to bottom, so distance, intermediate and near vision all sit on one lens. HOYA's progressives come in two design tiers:

### Personalized: MyStyle V+, Lifestyle 4X and the iD Series

Lenses in this tier are calculated from your individual prescription and fitting parameters. MyStyle V+ (listed on Chinese-language price lists as 金鑽頂級個人化) and Lifestyle 4X (銀鑽個人化 4X) sit here, and so does iD MySelf, the personalized version in the iD series. The fitting parameters can only be measured once your frame has been chosen and is on your face, so the lens order naturally comes after you pick a frame.

### Standard Designs: Balansis, Dynamic, VisuPro, AMP+

Balansis, Dynamic Premium and Dynamic Summit, VisuPro and AMP+ are designed around a standard wearing position. People getting their first progressives with a straightforward prescription often start choosing from this tier.

The two corridor designs are compared point by point in [ZEISS vs HOYA progressive corridors](/blog/progressive-corridor-brands/) (in Chinese).

## HOYA Indoor, Reading and Driving Lenses

Some people spend most of their day looking at just a few fixed distances. HOYA has separate series for this kind of use:

### Indoor Progressives: WorkStyle 3, Business, WorkSmart

These three series put the power zones at intermediate and near distances, so the computer and the documents on your desk are covered by the same lens. They give up the distance zone to make room, so these aren't glasses for driving or walking around.

### Single Vision With a Near-Support Design: Sync III

Sync III is a single-vision lens with a small amount of extra plus power built into the lower part. From the outside it looks like an ordinary single-vision lens, with none of a progressive's corridor. Whether it suits you depends on how long you spend at near distances each day and how close you hold things, which is assessed during your eye exam.

### Reading: SupeReader

SupeReader concentrates its power zone at near distance. Its job is reading books and documents.

### Driving: EnRoute

The EnRoute driving lenses, in progressive (PAL) and single-vision (SV) versions, come in refractive indices of 1.60 and 1.67. Where your eyes go while driving is different from the rest of the day; how to fit lenses for it is covered separately in [Oncoming Headlights Glaring at Night?](/en/blog/driving-lens-night-glare/)

## The Catalog Shows You the Series Name, Not Your Fitting Parameters

Every shop works from the same catalog. The differences only start to show once the lenses are on your face.

Take the same Lifestyle 4X fitted at two different shops. The lens itself is identical; what differs is the set of numbers entered when the order was placed, and whether the frame is still sitting where it was measured after you pick it up — where the fitting height was marked, and whether the optical centers line up with your pupils. If a number is off, the lens is calculated for that off position, and the finer the design, the more easily you'll feel the error. That's why premium series demand more of the measurements than entry-level ones, not less.

Choosing HOYA works the same way: get clear on what the glasses are for and which distances matter, and the list of series names shrinks to two or three on its own. What separates those last two or three is how completely the measurements are done.

**Q: How is a HOYA indoor lens different from a regular single-vision lens?**

A regular single-vision lens has one power across the whole lens, aimed at one distance. Indoor progressives (WorkStyle 3, Business, WorkSmart) put the power zones at intermediate and near distances, so one pair covers both the screen and the documents on your desk — but the distance zone has been given up, so they aren't suitable for driving. They come up most often with people who already have presbyopia and spend most of the day at a desk.
